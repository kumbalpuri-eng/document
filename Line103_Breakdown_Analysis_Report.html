2. Scope, Definitions & Data Overview

2.1Objective

The user has reported that Line 103 has been chronically unstable, with new problems arising almost every other day. The objective of this analysis is to (i) establish a quantified baseline of line behaviour, (ii) determine whether the perceived instability is supported by data and what is driving it, (iii) identify the silent wins — stations where the maintenance team has successfully stabilised the line — so that the same approach can be replicated elsewhere, (iv) forecast near-term behaviour, and (v) recommend a prioritised action plan with estimated impact.

3. Data Quality Checks

Before any conclusions, the raw data was passed through a structured quality pipeline.

Cleaning Rules Applied

  1. Date parsing — All breakdown and completion dates parsed using day-first format.
  2. Time parsing — 12-hour AM/PM source format normalised to internal timestamps.
  3. Duration derivation — Actual_Time (a datetime.time object) converted to minutes; where zero but timestamps differed, re-derived from timestamps.
  4. Fault type standardisation — Free-text labels normalised into a controlled vocabulary.
  5. Station number normalisation — Variants like 'OP--40', 'OP-40' standardised to 'ST-40' (Station 40).
  6. Shift assignment — Corrected to the A/B/C definitions above (07:30-16:00 etc.).
  7. Severity classification — Each event bucketed into Zero / Minor / Moderate / Major / Critical.
Residual Issue 1 — Memo/repeat entries: ~10.6% of records carry 0-min duration. These are 'Repeated memo entry' instances — the operator re-logs an open BRN slip rather than raising a new fault. They inflate count but not downtime. That's why we report both count-based and downtime-based views throughout.
Residual Issue 2 — Free-text fault descriptions: Spelling inconsistencies (e.g., 'trail' vs 'trial', 'kinfe' vs 'knife'). Text-mining results are directional rather than exact.

★ Positive Insights & Maintenance Wins

This section surfaces the silent wins — statistically verified evidence that the maintenance team's interventions have measurably reduced breakdown frequency on specific stations and eliminated specific recurring fault signatures. These wins deserve as much attention as the open pain points.

Headline:

P.1Stations With Statistically Improving Trends

For each station, a linear regression was fit on monthly breakdown count against time. A negative slope indicates the breakdown count is decreasing month-over-month. The p-value tells us whether the trend is statistically significant (p<0.05 = strong evidence of a real trend, not just noise).

P.2Fault Signatures Successfully Resolved

A "fault signature" is the combination of station + fault description. A signature is marked "Resolved" if it occurred at least twice historically (i.e., it was a recurring issue) but has not appeared in the last 45 days. This means the maintenance team's corrective action actually held — the same fault has stopped repeating.

P.3Weibull β < 1 — Reliability Bathtub Curve

Statistical evidence the line is in the "infant mortality" phase: The Weibull shape parameter β = (less than 1). In reliability engineering, β<1 means the rate of failures is decreasing over time — the line is in the burn-in / infant-mortality phase of the reliability bathtub curve. While the line still has many breakdowns, the trend of failures-per-unit-time is downward, indicating that as components and assemblies stabilise, fewer new failure modes are appearing.

P.4Shift Performance — Night Shift is Not the Worst

With the corrected shift definitions (A: 07:30-16:00, B: 16:00-00:30, C: 00:30-07:30), the per-shift-hour breakdown rate reveals a positive insight that the original assumption missed: Shift C (night) does not have the worst breakdown rate. The day shift (A) actually has the highest count. This means the perception of "night shift problems" was likely an artefact of the previous shift definition and not a real difference in operator performance.

! Pain Areas Still Open

Stations and fault signatures where the trend is worsening or the same fault keeps recurring despite interventions. These are the priority targets for the next round of root-cause analysis.

X.1Stations With Worsening Trends

These stations have a positive slope on monthly breakdown count — the breakdowns are increasing, not decreasing. Even if the p-value is not below 0.05 (small sample size), the direction is concerning and warrants investigation.

X.2Still-Active Recurring Fault Signatures

These are the fault signatures that appeared at least twice historically AND have appeared in the last 45 days. The maintenance team's corrective actions on these faults are not holding — the same fault is returning. These are the truest pain points: each entry in the table below is a fault the team has tried to fix and failed to fix permanently.

X.3Stations With Highest 24-Hour Recurrence

The recurrence probability within 24 hours is the share of breakdowns on a station that were followed by another breakdown on the same station within 24 hours. High recurrence = the team is fixing symptoms, not root causes.
